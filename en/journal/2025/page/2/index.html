The week starts not with a cup of coffee, but with a mug of hot shou puer tea.
For now, I’m trying to find the right balance in terms of the amount of tea, water, and steeping time.
But overall, this morning ritual pleases me. If only the heat would subside.

#11
21 июля

“…quench your thirst for bookish knowledge.”

It seems this is from Marcus Aurelius’s “Meditations,” written during the reign of the Roman Empire.

I bought his work a couple of days ago, on my way back from meeting an old friend.
I noticed that I read a lot, but I rarely put what I’ve read into practice. I’m just storing up knowledge.
Aurelius’s thought came to me just when I was wondering how to fix it, weary under the weight of the mindlessly accumulated ideas, notes, and quotes I’d pulled from books.

#10
11 июня

A cool breeze, howling like a seasoned hooligan, burst into the room. It creaked the open window sashes, spun the blades of the turned‑off fan, leafed through the pages of a carelessly tossed book, and, grabbing hold of the curtains, jumped from the windowsill into the bouquet of dried flowers. The dry leaves rustled, the vase swayed heavily and collapsed downward, freeing the top shelf of the bookshelf.
“Step aside!” “She warned in a low voice, shaking her mass of dry curls.
Someone darted to the side, spilling tea; someone slipped to the side, ducked, jumped back.
The stand made of fired clay caught the heavy weight of the vase. It crunched, ringing as it shattered into large fragments.
The wind picked up the fallen leaves of the dried flowers and, pleased with itself, slipped away through the open window.

#8
27 апреля

With the arrival of spring, this city is imbued with the dry scent of dust and smoke. Sometimes it seems to me that coming back here was a futile endeavor. It didn’t change anything. It only delayed the approach of the inevitable downfall we’ve been rushing towards for the past few years. It’s even interesting to wonder how much longer this agony will last and how it will ultimately end. Of course, I’d like to go back and do everything over again. But that’s the main curse of time — it moves only forward.

I guess it’s kind of scary, if not downright frightening, to give shape to the thoughts that race through my head throughout the day. Even on paper. That’s why I don’t write much. I just push them back and forth, without really focusing on any one of them. And they scatter in different directions, leaving only a long, dark shadow. And it’s unbearably unsettling to be in that shadow.
